Daniel's Onymous Omlette

Introduction

Do you need a warm, tasty snack in the morning that's simple, quick and satisfying to make? Daniel's Omynous Omlette can be just that, with my own spin on this classic morning dish. The first ever Omlette is believed to have originated in ancient Persia and has pretty much stayed the same throughout human history. It's a great dish that's also customizable to your liking, as you can add various fruits, vegetables or other flavouring to spice things up!

I like the Omlette as a morning dish because of its simplicity. It's a no-brainer to make in the morning when you're tired and isn't a particularly large meal, which makes it nice to eat.

Ingedients

  • 4 Eggs (Brown or white can work, but I prefer brown eggs)
  • ¾ Cups of Milk, preferably 3.5% fat. You can adjust the amount of milk you want to get your preferred taste.
  • Any other vegetable or fruit you would like to add, for this example I'll be using 3 cherry tomatoes.
  • Some salt
  • Your preferred quantity of pepper (optional)

Instructions

Prep Time: ~7 minutes / Cook Time: ~10 minutes / Total Time: ~17 minutes / Servings: 2.

Before starting: Put the 4 eggs into a bowl and wash them before use, as they need to be clean to avoid any infections. Grab a medium-sized frying pan and preheat it at medium heat, and make sure to add some olive oil to it to avoid the Omlette sticking to it. If you are adding any additional fruits or vegetables, wash and cut them now and leave them to the side.

Step 1: Crack the eggs into a large bowl and whisk them until the yolks all mix together into a yellow liquid.

Step 2: Add the ¾ Cups of 3.5% milk, and keep whisking to mix the milk in with the eggs, and the liquid should turn slightly more light-beige once you're done.

Step 3: When all mixed, add a sprinkle of salt to the liquid to give it a touch of flavor and mix it in. You can also add some pepper if you want at this time.

Step 4: Before pouring your Omlette into the pan, place any additions onto the already hot pan, like the cherry tomatoes you cut before. Once ready, pour the liquid into the pan, and it will partially cover the tomatoes. Put a cover on it and let it cook until the surface no longer looks liquid, you can check this by gently tilting the pan and seeing if the Omlette is still liquid.

Step 5: Once done, cut the Omlette in half whilst still on the pan and then remove each piece onto a plate. Put on any final toppings you may want and serve!
