History and Impact of Doctor Who

The very first episode of Doctor Who aired on BBC in 1963. It was created with the intent of being an educational family show, that would teach kids about science and history through fun stories about space and time travel. It became extremely popular very quickly, the eccentric and whimsical personality of The Doctor capturing audiences young and old alike, and remained so for a good 25 years.

Some of the older episodes of Doctor Who that aired during the 1960's.

Due to a combination of factors, the show became gradually less popular around 1989, leading to the BBC cancelling it; albeit with a promise that it would return. And return it did- in 2005, after a 16-year production hiatus. The new revival was welcomed by audiences with the same enthusiasm as it had gotten in the past, with the airing of the first episode receiving an average audience of 10.81 million viewers. And since then, the 21st-Century era of Doctor Who has received high ratings and glowing praise with consistency for the past almost 20 years that it's been running, garnering the highest audience appreciation index in the U.K. of any drama on television.

A fan poses next to a TARDIS replica in London, and a Dalek spotted at an Ottawa Comic Con.

The impact of Doctor Who on culture in the U.K. cannot be overstated. The show has featured various renowned actors over the years, and has been described as "quintessential to being British". But it's impact has since spread to audiences far beyond the United Kingdom, with its 50th Anniversary episode in 2013 being broadcast in 94 countries and screened to more than half a million people in cinemas. The scope of that broadcast set a new world record!
