The TARDIS

The TARDIS might singlehandedly be the most iconic and recognizable symbol of Doctor Who. Even if you've never seen or even heard of the show, you've probably seen the image of a blue police box somewhere. But, what exactly is it, and where did it come from?

The Beginning

The basic concept of the TARDIS is a spaceship that travels anywhere in time, as well as space. The TARDIS that The Doctor uses is one they stole from their home planet of Gallifrey, when they ran away thousands of years ago.

An older version of the TARDIS.

Technically, a TARDIS is meant to be able to assume any image from the outside to blend in with it's surroundings, thanks to something called a chameleon circuit. But the chameleon circuit in The Doctor's TARDIS is damaged, and so it's stuck in the form of an old-fashioned police box. A notable signature feature of the TARDIS is that it is bigger on the inside than it is on the outside.

The Tenth Doctor's TARDIS.

The actual name TARDIS is an acronym, standing for Time And Relative Dimension In Space. Each Doctor's TARDIS is unique to them, with vastly different interior designs reflecting each era and character of the show.

The Twelfth Doctor's TARDIS.

Fun Fact: In the U.K., where the show is most popular, there are actually many life size (from the outside, of course) replicas of the TARDIS built by fans across the country. There's even a website dedicated to the hobby of replicating the TARDIS!
