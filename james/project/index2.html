iPhone History

Mac OS X

Released on March 24, 2001, Apple's first operating system known for its elegant graphical interface. Introduced in 2001, Mac OS X represent a significant evolution to previous Apple products, such as other Macintosh's. Combining advanced technology at the time to a complex degree, yet a simple user interface that Apple user's are with known to today with their Apple products.

Building blocks for the iPhone iOS

Mac OS X provided the technology to backup the iOS, the iPhone operating system. Apple's iOS inherited similar features to Mac OS X, such as the user interface, and frameworks such as Core Graphics, Core Animation, and Core Audio, which provided a better iPhone experience. In addition, the point of the iPhone was to combine a music player, telephone, and a web browser/computer like Mac OS X.

Security

Apple, being known for their security, implemented a similar security system to the iPhone. Still to this day, the security system on both the iPhone and Mac are very much similar. Features like sandboxing, secure boot, and data encryption were crucial in ensuring the security of user’s data.

iPod

Released on October 23, 2001, the iPod was a portable media player that changed the way we listen to music. The iPod featuring simple interface, storage and the integration of iTunes.

Miniaturization and Portable Computing

In earlier days, listening to music wasn't always as portable. The use of CD's, cassette tapes, vinyl records were big bulky devices, and the Sony Walkman had featured a portable radio, though like the others, it was bulky and had to carry additional physical media. With the invention of the iPod, music could be way more on the go, with you only having to simply download the music onto the iPod with the use of a PC.

User Interface and Interaction Design

Apple implementing the click-scroll wheel was a major influence to iPhone's digital keyboard. It had allowed simple access to music libraries and songs, with just the click of a button. This seamless feature influenced the development and creation of the iPhone's touchscreen interface. Apple had used its new way of interacting to music and had put it towards how we interact with smart phones with the multi-touch interface.



Early models of the iPhone



With several products leading up to the iPhone, on January 9th, 2007, Steve Jobs announces the iPhone. With several people being amazed, and some being confused and disappointed. The idea of the iPhone was remarkable, compacting several products into one, and making it in the spotlight compared to other brands such as Nokia, Blackberry, etc. Yet, some thought that a product like this wouldn't succeed. In 2007, when the blackberry CEO was interviewed, he had said that the iPhone "lacks the functionality of the BlackBerry", relating to the fact that the iPhone doesn't have a physical keyboard, unlike the BlackBerry. Despite the conflict on whether or not the iPhone would succeed and compete with its competitors, the iPhone had marked a significant shift in consumer interest to a more futuristic touchscreen, with a keyboard that changes depending on the app. This ultimately shaped the Phone industry to how it is today, with companies like BlackBerry and Nokia facing a severe decline since. Even when those companies had followed Apple's ideas, there was no need for consumers to switch back to their original cellphones, as Apple had already advanced in another category.
